### 4. Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation/growth/labor:** August nonfarm payrolls blew past expectations (+162K vs. ~54–56K consensus), reigniting inflation/overheating concerns and flipping the rate-path debate toward a possible hike rather than a cut. Friday's CPI print (Sept 11) is now the key confirming/disconfirming data point.
- **Geopolitics:** Active, escalating US–Iran conflict around the Strait of Hormuz — US forces disabled/destroyed three Iranian oil tankers over the weekend after Iran's IRGC fired ballistic missiles toward a US carrier and destroyer and struck three tankers/three US-linked vessels. Iran's chief negotiator says "the era of proportionate responses is over," raising the risk of further escalation. This is keeping a geopolitical premium in oil (Brent near $97, approaching $100) and lifting defense names.
- **Trade policy:** The US–Canada tariff war escalated further today as Canada's 15%/25%/50% retaliatory tariffs on ~$27.6B of US goods (steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp/paper, electronics) take effect, doubling steel/aluminum duties to 50% in response to the US's 50% tariff on Canadian goods (hockey equipment, cement, liquor, dairy) since Aug 22. Ottawa has pledged ~$7.5B in fresh support (on top of ~$25B already committed) for affected industries.
- **Micro:** Defense/aerospace (LMT, RTX, GE Aerospace) benefiting from Iran-conflict-driven restocking demand. Memory/storage (SanDisk and peers) rallying on a structural AI-driven NAND shortage and long-term hyperscaler capacity deals. Autos/EV in focus after Tesla's post-Cybercab slide. Energy names mixed despite higher crude — XOM and CVX both traded lower Monday even as oil rose, suggesting profit-taking/rotation rather than a clean energy-sector bid.
