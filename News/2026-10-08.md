### Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** Core PCE cooled slightly to 3.0% y/y in August, but the broader 2026 inflation trend had been firm enough to prompt the Fed's first hike since 2023 in September. Energy-driven inflation risk tied to the roughly seven-month Strait of Hormuz disruption remains the dominant upside risk, having driven the 10-year Treasury yield to its highest level since 2002.
- **Growth / labor:** September payrolls rose just ~29,000 versus ~85-90K consensus; unemployment rose to 4.2%; July and August were revised down by a combined ~60K. This labor-market softening — arriving after the Fed's September hike — is the main reason October hike odds have fallen even as the yield structure stays historically elevated. Next real tests: today's jobless claims, then CPI/retail sales on Oct 14–15.

US government funding is a non-issue through Dec 11, 2026 under the September continuing resolution.
