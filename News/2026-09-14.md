### Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** The narrative remains "will the Fed hike," not "when does it cut." August CPI came in hot, and now a fresh oil shock is layering additional cost-push pressure on top of an already-hawkish Fed just two days before its decision — an unusual and difficult setup (tightening into a supply shock).
- **Growth/labor:** Labor market still described as resilient/stable (unemployment ~4.1%), which is precisely what's giving the Fed room to consider hiking rather than easing.
- **Geopolitics:** The dominant cross-asset driver. The wider 2026 Iran war has left the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed; Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline — its main alternative crude-export route (~5mbd capacity) — was shut after Sept 10-11 drone strikes from Iraqi territory, with reports Aramco refining infrastructure was also hit. Saudi Arabia reportedly has only days of export stock before the lost capacity (up to 4% of global supply) becomes a hard constraint.
- **Policy:** A genuinely hawkish Fed under new Chair Kevin Warsh — a sharp reversal from cut-era expectations earlier in 2026 — is now on a collision course with a geopolitically-driven oil spike, raising the odds of a "hike into a shock" outcome this week.
- **Micro / sector rotation:** Energy is the clear relative winner (oil majors bid on both sides of the Atlantic); AI/software and chip names are under pressure on the OpenAI-vs-Anthropic IPO divergence and broader AI-safety headline risk even as some individual semis (ADI, ORCL, QCOM, INTC) bounced on stock-specific strength Friday/this morning; defense names are sensitive to any further Middle East escalation headlines; gold and silver — despite the war backdrop — are falling as hike-driven real-yield and dollar strength dominates safe-haven demand.
