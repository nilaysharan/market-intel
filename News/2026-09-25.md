### Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation/growth:** The core tension remains "good news is bad news" - strong business-activity/PMI data keeps the Fed in hiking mode even as war-driven energy costs (diesel at record highs, Brent near $107) squeeze consumers and businesses directly. Consumer sentiment (UMich prelim 47.8) reflects that squeeze.
- **Geopolitics/policy:** The 2026 Iran war is in its 7th month (began Feb 28). An April ceasefire mediated by Pakistan and a June memorandum of understanding both collapsed by July after Iran struck commercial vessels bypassing its approved Strait of Hormuz route; Hormuz traffic has fallen to a handful of vessels per day versus pre-war norms. A Saudi pipeline strike has added further supply pressure. The White House is weighing a 90-day diesel export ban; the EU has called it a "bad idea," and US business groups (Chamber of Commerce, Business Roundtable) warned it could backfire by ultimately pushing fuel prices higher as refiners cut output.
- **Micro/sector rotation:** This week's pattern (per Thursday's tape) has generally favored energy, select industrials, and AI-capex-linked chip/power names, while rate-sensitive groups (financials, software, travel/leisure) and some AI-debt-sensitive megacaps have lagged. AMD's crossing of a $1T market cap is the standout single-stock story of the week; MGM's failed buyout and McDonald's' investor-day selloff are the other notable idiosyncratic moves.
