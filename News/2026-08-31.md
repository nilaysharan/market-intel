### 4. Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** July PCE (released late Aug) came in hot and unchanged from June — headline 3.7% YoY, core 3.3% YoY (vs. 2% target), core monthly pace accelerating to 0.2% from 0.1%. This is the data Warsh cited as evidence the Fed still has "work to do" — the crux of the hawkish repricing.
- **Growth/labor:** July nonfarm payrolls unexpectedly fell 23,000 (vs. ~+80,000 consensus), driven by a 53,000 drop in government jobs plus retail/leisure softness; May was revised down 66,000 to net +63,000. Unemployment ticked down to 4.1%, but only because labor-force participation fell to 61.4% — a five-year low. Average hourly earnings growth slowed to 3.2% YoY, the softest since May 2021. Net: a labor market weakening even as inflation stays sticky — the classic hard case for the Fed, and why Friday's jobs report carries outsized weight.

Separately, Canada-US tariff retaliation continues, and Treasury has been expanding Iran-related sanctions. Qualcomm's CEO is joining a US business delegation for a Trump-Xi summit trip to China, with chip-export rules and market access on the agenda — a swing factor for chip-sector sentiment (AMD's thesis flagged by analysts as sensitive to any tightening of China export rules).
