### Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** July PCE data (released late Aug) came in hotter than expected and unchanged from June — headline PCE 3.7% YoY, core PCE 3.3% YoY (vs. 2% target), core monthly pace accelerating to 0.2% from 0.1%. This is the data Warsh cited as evidence the Fed still has "work to do," and it's the crux of the hawkish repricing.
- **Growth/labor:** July nonfarm payrolls unexpectedly fell 23,000 (vs. consensus of roughly +80,000), driven by a 53,000 drop in government jobs plus softness in retail, leisure/hospitality; May payrolls were revised down 66,000 to a net +63,000. Unemployment ticked down to 4.1%, but only because labor-force participation fell further to 61.4% — a five-year low. Average hourly earnings growth slowed to 3.2% YoY, the softest since May 2021. Net effect: a labor market that is weakening even as inflation stays sticky — the classic hard case for the Fed, and the reason Friday's jobs report carries outsized weight.
- **Geopolitics/policy:** Canada announced retaliatory tariffs matching the US's 50% levies dollar-for-dollar amid failed US-Canada trade talks; the Treasury (Secretary Bessent) has been expanding sanctions on Iran; Middle East tensions remain elevated. Separately, Qualcomm's CEO is joining a US business delegation for a Trump-Xi summit trip to China, with semiconductor export rules and market access on the agenda — a swing factor for chip-sector sentiment (AMD's thesis in particular is flagged by analysts as sensitive to any tightening of China export rules).
- **Micro:** Sector leadership rotated sharply toward Energy (+12.1% in August, on rising oil prices) and Financials (+6.2%) on the month, while Information Technology has been the standout laggard (-8.0% in August) despite Nvidia's earnings beat, alongside Industrials (-2.9%) and Utilities (-2.2%). On Friday specifically, Energy (+0.59%), Consumer Staples (+0.55%) and Financials (+0.34%) outperformed while Info Tech (-1.29%), Utilities (-1.14%) and Industrials (-0.96%) lagged — consistent with a "sell the hawkish Fed read, rotate defensive/value" session.
