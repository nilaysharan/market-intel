### Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation/growth:** September's flash S&P Global Composite PMI jumped to 58.4 (from 56.0), the highest since July 2021, with input-cost inflation at its highest since October 2022 and supply-chain delays the most widespread since mid-2022 - a "good news is bad news" print that reinforces the Fed's hiking bias rather than easing rate pressure.
- **Labor/consumer:** Preliminary September University of Michigan sentiment plunged 7.5% month-over-month to 47.8, the second-lowest reading on record, with year-ahead inflation expectations surging to 4.6% - consumers are increasingly worried about rising fuel prices and trade tensions squeezing household budgets even as headline business activity accelerates. This growth/sentiment divergence is the core "stagflation" story shaping markets this week.
- **Geopolitics/policy:** The 2026 Iran war remains the dominant tail risk. President Trump told the UN General Assembly he has a "big decision" between negotiating with Iran or moving to "annihilate" it, and separately indicated any durable peace deal likely waits until after the Nov. 3 US midterms - markets read this as removing near-term de-escalation hope, keeping an elevated risk premium in oil. Iranian President Pezeshkian's defiant UN speech blamed the US and Israel for global instability.
- **Micro/sector rotation:** Rate-sensitive and duration-heavy groups (brokers/financials via NIM and business-model disruption fears - SCHW -6.1%; enterprise software - INTU -3.9%, CSCO -4.5%; travel - BKNG -4.4%; mega-cap AI capex skeptics - GOOGL -3.7%) led losses, while select industrials (HON +2.6%, DE +2.4%, BA +2.3%), chips (MU +5.0%, CDNS +2.7%), and utilities/power names tied to AI data-center demand (CEG +2.9%) outperformed - a mixed, stock-specific tape layered on top of the broad rates-driven selloff.
