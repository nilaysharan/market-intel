### 4. Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation/growth:** University of Michigan September consumer sentiment printed 48.1 (final), a four-month low and down ~15% from January, as 1-year inflation expectations jumped from 4.0% to 4.6% (highest since June) and 5-year expectations rose to 3.4%. The Fed's hiking cycle (started Sept 16) is a direct response to this inflation-expectations drift, not a growth-driven move - markets are pricing near-certainty of continued tightening into October.
- **Geopolitics/policy:** The US-Iran conflict remains the dominant macro risk. Iran proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz within a week if seven conditions were met (lifting the blockade, releasing frozen funds, ending attacks); Trump rejected the plan over the weekend as unacceptable and reiterated threats against Tehran. The US military is now escorting tankers through Hormuz in daylight, with outbound crude volume near 13M bbl/day (roughly double a month ago). Any escalation/de-escalation headline remains the top swing factor for oil and risk sentiment this week.
