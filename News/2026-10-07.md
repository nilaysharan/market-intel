### Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** Energy-driven inflation concerns — tied to the roughly seven-month Strait of Hormuz disruption — have been the dominant market story, pushing the 10-year Treasury yield to its highest level since 2002 and the 30-year above a 24-year high in late September/early October. Tuesday's easing in yields and oil offered some relief but the level remains historically elevated.
- **Growth / labor:** September payrolls rose just ~29,000 versus ~85-90K consensus; unemployment rose to 4.2%; July and August were revised down by a combined ~60K. This is the main reason October hike odds have fallen from their late-September peak even as the broader yield structure stays elevated. Next data test: today's FOMC minutes, then CPI/retail sales on Oct 14-15.
- **Geopolitics / policy:** The Iran/Strait of Hormuz situation remains a live overhang — an earlier ceasefire did not fully resolve the conflict, and bypass pipelines through Saudi Arabia/UAE have capped the net global oil-supply loss even as disruption persists. US government funding is a non-issue through Dec 11, 2026 under the September continuing resolution.
- **Micro / sector rotation:** The AI-capex/memory trade (semiconductor and memory names) has been the single biggest driver of index-level gains for weeks, even as several rate-sensitive sectors (banks, REITs, utilities) and some consumer-discretionary/communications names have lagged on the higher-for-longer yield backdrop. Watch whether Tuesday's yield relief broadens participation beyond AI hardware as Q3 earnings season approaches.
