### Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** September CPI (released Oct 14) ran hot — headline **3.7% y/y** (vs. ~3.6% expected), core **2.6% y/y / +0.33% m/m** (vs. ~2.5%/+0.2% expected) — with gasoline (+9.1% m/m, tied to the Hormuz-driven oil shock) accounting for more than half the headline upside. This is the proximate driver of the hawkish repricing into the Oct 28 FOMC decision.
- **Growth / labor:** September payrolls were very weak (~29K vs. ~85-90K consensus, unemployment 4.2%, with downward revisions to prior months) — a genuinely two-sided picture (soft jobs vs. hot, energy-driven inflation) heading into next week's decision. A January 2026 partial government shutdown (funding lapse Jan 30-Feb) previously delayed BLS releases (the January jobs report slipped to Feb 11); Congress passed a stopgap in early September funding the government through **Dec 11, 2026**, reducing near-term shutdown risk but setting up another funding fight right after the FOMC's December meeting. BLS has also been without a Senate-confirmed commissioner and faces staffing/budget strain, a lingering data-quality/timeliness risk.
- **Geopolitics:** The US-Israel-Iran conflict (active since Feb 28, 2026) continues to flare around the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf shipping lanes. Ceasefire efforts have repeatedly broken down (an April ceasefire, the June 17 Islamabad Memorandum, and its July 8 collapse); the most recently reported attacks hit tankers near Al Jubayl, Saudi Arabia (Oct 17). This is the direct driver of Brent's climb toward $112/bbl, a 5-month high, and a persistent tail risk for energy, the dollar, and yields.
- **Micro / sector rotation:** The **"AI-capex discipline"** theme dominates equity positioning — Meta's new AI-funding stock class and higher capex guidance, Lam Research's weak outlook (following Applied Materials' no-forecast investor day), and Tesla's capex cut all re-opened the same AI-infrastructure-spending skepticism that drove the Oct 8-16 selloff. Expect the semiconductor/AI-infrastructure basket (NVDA, AMD, AVGO, MU, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, INTC, ORCL) to keep trading as a single group into next week's Microsoft/Alphabet/Amazon reports. Elsewhere: bank earnings (Oct 13-14) were mixed — JPMorgan missed (EPS $5.57 vs. $5.90 consensus), Goldman beat strongly (EPS $17.01, equities revenue +72% to $6.44bn, one of its best quarters ever), Citigroup beat on profit but fell on cautious Markets-revenue guidance, and Bank of America's profit was hit by a $1.2bn regulatory penalty.
