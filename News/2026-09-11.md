### 4. Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** The narrative has flipped from "rate cuts" to "will the Fed hike" over the past two weeks. Thursday's PPI (+0.4% m/m, +5.4% y/y — hotter than expected on the headline y/y print) pushed yields higher and stocks lower; today's CPI is the swing factor for whether that hike narrative solidifies further into next week's FOMC.
- **Growth/labor:** No fresh labor-market data overnight; Fed commentary continues to describe the labor market as comparatively "stable" relative to the inflation side of the mandate.
- **Geopolitics:** The US-Iran conflict is the dominant cross-asset driver right now — it's been running roughly six months and shows little sign of resolution. The latest escalation (destroyed Iranian tankers, retaliatory strikes on US assets, attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure by an Iran-backed group, and rising security risk around the Strait of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb) has pushed Brent up roughly 25% since early August and is the direct channel through which geopolitics is feeding into the inflation/Fed story.
- **Micro / sector rotation:** Energy majors (XOM, CVX) are outperforming on the oil spike while oilfield-services names (SLB) lag; Materials (XLB) has been notably weak this week on a copper/gold/silver pullback (FCX -7%); high-multiple AI/software and chip names have been under pressure into this week's close (Micron -4%, Vertiv/CoreWeave -5%, Lumentum -4% earlier in the week; chip stocks broadly soft again in Friday premarket on credit-cost concerns) even as mega-cap AI capex announcements (Meta's AI assistant, Google's Finland buildout) continue. Consumer Staples (XLP) has been one of the few relatively resilient sectors this week despite softness in food names. Healthcare was broadly firm Thursday (JNJ +1.7%, UNH +1.6%, ABBV +1.4%).
