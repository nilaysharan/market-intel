### 4. Macro backdrop (what's changed)
- **Inflation:** The dominant market story. A global bond sell-off intensified through late September into early October on hot energy-driven inflation prints and strong PMI/ISM data, pushing the 10-year to its highest since 2002 and the 30-year above 5.6% for the first time in 24 years. Energy-driven inflation (gasoline/diesel, tied to the Hormuz disruption) is the main culprit cited by multiple sources.
- **Growth/labor:** September payrolls rose just 29,000 versus ~84-90K consensus; unemployment rose to 4.2% (vs. 4.1% expected); July was revised to -10K and August revised down to 133K from 162K (a combined -60K revision); wage growth slowed to +0.1% m/m / +3% y/y, the softest annual pace since 2021. This print is the main reason October hike odds have come down from their late-September peak, even as the broader yield structure stays elevated.
- **Geopolitics/policy:** Treasury Secretary Bessent says Iran will have "no oil on the water" and "no revenues" from exports this week as the US pressure campaign intensifies; Washington is also pushing European allies to release strategic reserves and diesel stockpiles to cap fuel costs. The Strait of Hormuz situation has lasted roughly seven months now, but bypass infrastructure has kept the price shock more contained than initially feared. Government funding is a non-issue through Dec 11, 2026.
- **Micro:** The AI-capex/memory story (INTC, MU, KLAC, AMAT) remains the single biggest driver of index-level gains, pushing the Nasdaq to fresh records even as rate-sensitive sectors (banks, REITs, utilities) and several consumer-discretionary/communications names (SBUX, CMG, UPS, TMUS, NFLX, DIS) lagged Monday. This bifurcation — AI hardware up strongly, almost everything rate-sensitive or "old economy" down — is the clearest sector-rotation signal in the data.
